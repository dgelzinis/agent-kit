# Mode: page — one page that wins one search or question

For a new page or the rework of an existing one. The page is built through the normal flow; this mode produces its brief and checks the result.

1. **The target.** One primary question or search, the person asking it, and what they want to do next. Search the web for it: what the current top results and AI answers say, what they miss, what format wins (guide, comparison, tool, list).
2. **The promise.** What this page gives that the others do not: a clearer answer, real numbers, a worked example, a tool, an honest comparison.
3. **The brief** (posted on the issue; this is the design input for `/k-design` and the writing input for `/k-content`):
   - title and meta description (specific, the benefit first, no clickbait);
   - the `h1` and the outline, as the questions a reader has, in the order they have them;
   - **the direct answer in the first lines** (two or three sentences a person, a search engine or an AI system could quote as they stand), then the depth;
   - the proof: figures with sources and dates, product screens, examples;
   - comparisons and alternatives treated fairly, with facts;
   - a short question-and-answer block only for questions people really ask;
   - the one call to action, and internal links in and out;
   - structured data that matches what the page shows; canonical URL; `alt` texts.
4. **Build it** through the flow: words by `/k-content` in the project's voice, layout by `/k-design`, code by `/k-code` following the site's technology pack (server-rendered content, fast, accessible).
5. **Check before shipping.** The content is in the HTML without JavaScript; title, description, headings and structured data are right; the page is in the sitemap and linked from related pages; it is fast on a phone; every claim is true and sourced; nothing reads as written for a machine.
6. **After it is live** (owner's step): request indexing where the engines offer it, add the page to the plan's measures, and look at it again after a few weeks (`modes/measure.md`). Keep it current: a dated, updated page keeps its place; a stale one loses it.
