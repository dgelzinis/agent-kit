# Problem pack: being found — SEO and GEO

**Standards:** Google Search Essentials and the SEO Starter Guide (developers.google.com/search), including its guide to generative AI features in Search; Bing Webmaster Guidelines (bing.com/webmasters); schema.org for structured data; Core Web Vitals (web.dev/vitals). GEO (visibility in AI answers) is young and changes fast: prefer the engines' own documentation and independent studies over vendor claims, and check current guidance before acting.

## What holds for search engines and AI answers alike
- **Useful, original, specific content for a real person's question** is the main lever. Commodity summaries of what is already out there earn nothing.
- **It must be fetchable and readable.** Main content in the server's HTML (many AI crawlers run little or no JavaScript); public pages not blocked in `robots.txt`; a sitemap; correct canonicals and status codes; indexed in Google and in Bing (several AI assistants retrieve through Bing).
- **Answer first.** A question-shaped heading, the direct answer in two or three sentences, then the depth. Concrete numbers, definitions and examples rather than preamble.
- **Fast and stable on a phone;** accessible; HTTPS.
- **Clear identity and trust:** who is behind it, consistent name and facts everywhere, sources for claims, dates, and content kept current. This matters most for money, health and legal topics.
- **Earned presence elsewhere.** Being genuinely mentioned, reviewed, compared and linked on sites people trust is what makes engines and AI systems treat a brand as real. It cannot be faked safely.

## Page basics
- One topic and intent per page; unique `title` and meta description; one `h1`; headings that outline the answer.
- Descriptive URLs; internal links with meaningful text; no orphan pages.
- Images with width, height and `alt`; structured data (JSON-LD) only for what the page really shows, validated.
- International: one URL per language or market, `hreflang`, and real translation.

## GEO: what the evidence supports, and what it does not
- **Supported:** crawlability by AI crawlers, answer-first structure, fresh and dated content, original data and specifics, fair comparison pages, and authentic third-party presence.
- **Google's own position:** its AI features draw on the same index and ranking systems as Search; doing SEO well is how to appear there, and no special AI files or markup are required.
- **Unproven or contested, so never the plan:** `llms.txt` (harmless to publish, but do not expect results from it), structured data "for AI", rewriting or chunking content for machines, and any scheme to plant brand mentions.
- **Blocking AI crawlers** removes the site from AI answers; do it only on purpose.

## Never
Keyword stuffing, hidden text, doorway pages, bought or exchanged links, fake reviews or testimonials, planted mentions, scraped or mass-generated filler, misleading titles. They breach the engines' spam policies and the kit's rules on honesty.

## Measuring
Search console data (queries, pages, indexing); AI-assistant referrals segmented in analytics, and the engines' AI reports where offered; a fixed panel of target questions re-asked over time; conversions by landing page. Rankings and citations cannot be promised; direction and evidence can be shown.
